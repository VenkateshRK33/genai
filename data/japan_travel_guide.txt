TravelTruth AI - Japan Demo Knowledge Base

IMPORTANT:
This is a fictional demo knowledge base for a college project. It is not official
travel, visa, health, or safety advice. Users must verify current information with
official Japanese government, embassy, airline, and local authority sources.

MYTH: Japan is completely cashless.
FACT: Major cities and many businesses accept cards and mobile payments, but some
smaller restaurants, local shops, temples, and rural services may still require
cash. Travelers should carry some Japanese yen.

MYTH: Japan has one visa rule for every traveler.
FACT: Visa requirements depend on nationality, passport type, purpose, and length of
stay. Travelers should check the current requirements with the official Ministry of
Foreign Affairs of Japan website or their nearest Japanese embassy.

MYTH: Trains in Japan wait for late passengers.
FACT: Japanese trains generally operate on strict schedules. Travelers should arrive
at the platform early and check the station's signs and announcements.

MYTH: Tipping is required in Japan.
FACT: Tipping is generally not expected in restaurants, taxis, or hotels. Good service
is normally included in the price. Travelers should follow the establishment's
instructions rather than leaving money automatically.

MYTH: Eating and drinking while walking is always acceptable.
FACT: Local etiquette varies. In crowded areas, public transport, and some tourist
locations, eating or drinking while walking may be discouraged. Travelers should
observe local signs and customs.

TRAVEL CHECKLIST:
- Check passport validity and current visa requirements using official sources.
- Confirm airline baggage and entry requirements before departure.
- Save accommodation and emergency contact details offline.
- Carry a small amount of local currency for places that do not accept cards.
- Check weather, transport notices, and local advisories before each travel day.

TRAVEL PLANNING:
- Tokyo is useful for first-time visitors who want major city attractions, shopping,
  museums, and many train connections.
- Kyoto is known for temples, shrines, traditional neighborhoods, and seasonal
  scenery. Popular sites can be crowded, so early mornings may be quieter.
- Osaka is known for food, nightlife, and access to nearby destinations such as Nara.
- A first-time seven-day route might combine Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka, but travelers
  should avoid packing too many cities into one trip.
- Spring and autumn are popular travel seasons. Weather and crowd levels vary by
  region and year, so check current forecasts before booking activities.
- Summer can be hot and humid in many areas. Winter conditions vary by region, with
  heavier snow possible in northern and mountainous areas.

TRANSPORT:
- Japan has local trains, subways, buses, taxis, and long-distance rail services.
- Routes, fares, reservations, and operating times should be checked in a current
  navigation or railway app because schedules can change.
- A rechargeable transit card can be convenient where supported, but availability,
  payment methods, and refund rules should be checked at the relevant station.
- Some long-distance services require or strongly benefit from advance reservations,
  especially during holidays and peak seasons.
- Keep noise low on public transport, queue in marked areas, and avoid blocking
  doors or luggage spaces.

ACCOMMODATION AND PACKING:
- Confirm check-in time, luggage storage, cancellation rules, and the nearest station.
- Pack comfortable walking shoes, a compact umbrella, and clothing suitable for the
  season. Some traditional accommodations may have specific footwear expectations.
- Keep passport, insurance details, medicines, and emergency contacts accessible.
- Check whether your accommodation provides toiletries, adapters, laundry, and Wi-Fi.

MONEY AND CONNECTIVITY:
- Japan uses the yen (JPY). Exchange rates and card acceptance can change, so check
  current rates and keep a backup payment method.
- An eSIM, SIM, or pocket Wi-Fi can help with maps and translation. Confirm device
  compatibility and activation requirements before departure.
- Save hotel addresses in Japanese characters for showing to drivers or asking for
  directions.

EMERGENCY AND RESPONSIBLE TRAVEL:
- For emergencies, learn the current police, ambulance, fire, and multilingual
  support numbers from official Japanese sources before travel.
- Follow local warnings for earthquakes, typhoons, volcanic activity, heat, and
  heavy snow. Do not rely on an old social-media post.
- Respect restricted areas, photography rules, shrine and temple guidance, and
  neighborhood quiet hours.
